What Happened

Apple opened WWDC 2026 this week at Apple Park, and for the first time in years the keynote led with Siri rather than burying it. The headline: the long-promised overhaul of Siri is finally shipping — and it is powered in part by a custom Google Gemini model running on Apple's Private Cloud Compute servers, under a reported arrangement worth roughly $1 billion per year for a model in the neighborhood of 1.2 trillion parameters.

The key beats from the keynote:

  • The new Siri, first teased at WWDC 2024 and repeatedly delayed, now handles on-screen awareness, personal context, and multi-step "in-app actions" via App Intents — the agentic features Apple pulled from the original timeline.
  • iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27 and the rest of the lineup, continuing the year-based naming Apple adopted in 2025. Expect a fall release.
  • A second year of the Liquid Glass design language, refined after the readability complaints that dogged iOS 26's first builds.
  • An expanded Foundation Models framework, giving developers deeper access to Apple's on-device model — and, notably, to the larger server model — for free, with no per-token inference bill.
💡
The quiet bombshell: the most capable version of Siri does not run an Apple model. It runs a Google-built Gemini variant on Apple's own servers. Apple is positioning itself as the privacy-preserving front end; Google supplies the intelligence underneath.

Why It Matters

For three years Apple insisted its strategy was on-device, in-house AI. This keynote is a public admission that Apple's own large models weren't good enough to ship the assistant it promised — so it rented Google's. That reframes the competitive map.

Who wins: Google, first and most. Beyond the estimated $1 billion annually, the deal validates Gemini as the model others lean on when their own falls short — the same way the Google–Apple search default has anchored Google's position for years. Developers win too: a genuinely useful, free, locally-integrated model lowers the cost of adding AI features that previously meant an OpenAI or Anthropic API bill.

Who's exposed: Apple, reputationally. The "we build our own" narrative is gone, and regulators already scrutinizing the Google search-default payments now have a second multi-billion-dollar Apple–Google dependency to examine. OpenAI also loses a little shine — its ChatGPT integration in iOS remains, but it's no longer the marquee AI partner; that seat now belongs to Gemini for Siri's core reasoning.

Context

This caps a rough arc. At WWDC 2024 Apple unveiled "Apple Intelligence" and a personalized Siri it couldn't ship; by spring 2025 the company publicly conceded the assistant was delayed, and a leadership shake-up moved Siri under the Vision Pro team. WWDC 2025 leaned on the Liquid Glass redesign and year-based names precisely because the Siri story wasn't ready.

The Gemini partnership, first reported in late 2025, is the resolution. It mirrors a broader industry pattern: the companies that own distribution (Apple's billion-plus active devices) increasingly buy frontier capability from the handful of labs that own the models. Samsung already ships Gemini-backed Galaxy AI; Apple joining means Google's models now sit behind the assistants on the two largest phone platforms on earth.

💡
Privacy framing to watch: Apple stresses that the Gemini model runs on its own Private Cloud Compute infrastructure, not Google's servers, with no data retention. Whether that distinction satisfies privacy advocates — and regulators — is the open question of the next year.

What To Do Next

For iPhone users: don't rush. The new Siri features roll out in phases through the iOS 27 cycle, and first-week betas are historically rough. If you rely on your phone daily, wait for the public beta or the fall release. When it lands, revisit Siri's privacy settings and the ChatGPT/third-party extension toggles — the defaults will change.

For developers: this is the actionable part. Audit your app's App Intents now — that's how the new Siri discovers and triggers your features, and apps that expose clean intents will surface in the assistant while competitors stay invisible. Then evaluate the expanded Foundation Models framework: for summarization, extraction, and classification, the free on-device model can replace paid API calls outright. Prototype against it before renewing an inference contract.

For buyers and IT teams: the heaviest Siri features require Apple Intelligence-capable hardware (A17 Pro / M-series and newer). If a fleet refresh was already on the roadmap, this is a reason to confirm timing rather than accelerate it. And model the dependency risk: an assistant strategy now resting on an Apple–Google commercial deal is one antitrust ruling away from renegotiation.

Watch the fall release notes and the regulatory filings. The technology shipped this week; the harder questions — about who owns the intelligence in your pocket, and what that costs — are just starting.
