What Happened

SpaceX is reportedly preparing to go public — and the early framing is less about a stock ticker than about a single line on a net-worth chart. Multiple outlets today led with the same claim: a SpaceX IPO would push Elon Musk past $1 trillion, making him the world's first trillionaire. Chinese tech wire 36Kr carried the same item near the top of its June 7 roundup ("SpaceX据悉将其IPO…"), confirming the news is moving across markets, not just U.S. tech press.

The concrete facts, as reported, are narrow but significant:

  • The vehicle: Reports point to SpaceX itself, with Starlink — its satellite-internet unit — as the value engine investors are actually buying. Starlink now operates roughly 7,000+ satellites and serves several million subscribers across more than 100 countries.
  • The valuation backdrop: SpaceX was last marked near $350 billion in a December 2024 tender offer, with secondary trades since reportedly pushing it past $400 billion. A public listing would likely re-rate it well above that.
  • Control: Musk holds roughly 42% of SpaceX and, more importantly, super-voting control. Any IPO structure is expected to preserve that grip rather than dilute it.

What is not confirmed: a price range, a share count, or a date. Treat "trillionaire" as a headline-math projection — Musk's stake multiplied by an aspirational public valuation — not a filed number.
