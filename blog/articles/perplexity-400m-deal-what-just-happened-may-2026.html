Perplexity $400M Deal: What Just Happened (May 2026)

What Happened

Snap and Perplexity have "amicably ended" the $400 million distribution deal the two companies announced in November 2025, according to a statement from Snap confirmed today. Under the original agreement, Perplexity was set to pay Snap $400 million — a mix of cash and equity — over one year, in exchange for embedding Perplexity's AI answer engine directly inside the Snapchat chat interface for the platform's roughly 943 million monthly active users. That integration, which had been targeted for a global rollout in early 2026, will no longer ship.

Neither company has disclosed which side initiated the wind-down, the size of any breakup payment, or whether portions of the cash had already been transferred. Snap's framing — "amicably ended" — is the only on-record characterization so far. Perplexity has not issued a substantive public statement beyond confirming the deal is off.

Key facts on the table:

  • Original deal value: $400M over 12 months, announced November 2025
  • Distribution surface lost: in-chat AI inside Snapchat (≈943M MAU)
  • Status: terminated by mutual agreement; no replacement partner announced
  • Timing: roughly six months after signing, before the integration shipped to general availability

Why It Matters

This is the largest publicly known AI distribution deal to collapse before launch, and the implications cut in three directions.

For Perplexity, losing Snapchat removes its single biggest planned consumer-distribution channel. The company has been racing to convert search-query volume into a defensible moat against Google's AI Overviews and OpenAI's ChatGPT search, and Snapchat would have plugged it directly into a Gen Z audience that neither competitor dominates inside a messaging surface. Without it, Perplexity's distribution story leans more heavily on its standalone app, the Comet browser, and its enterprise tier.

For Snap, the company forfeits what would have been a meaningful, near-term revenue line — $400M is roughly 7-8% of Snap's annual revenue — but regains optionality. Snap's existing My AI feature runs on third-party models, and ending the exclusivity gives Snap room to negotiate with OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google, or to lean further into its own in-house generative stack.

For the broader market, the unwind is a data point that the 2025 wave of nine- and ten-figure "AI-inside-the-app" distribution deals are harder to operationalize than the press releases suggested. Product fit, revenue share mechanics, and brand-safety constraints don't always survive contact with implementation.

💡
Read the signal, not the spin. "Amicably ended" almost always means one party wanted out badly enough that the other accepted terms to avoid litigation. Watch the next 60 days for who signs a replacement deal first — that's the side that was looking to leave.

Context

The Snap–Perplexity deal landed in November 2025 during a frenzied stretch of AI distribution dealmaking. In the same six-month window, OpenAI deepened its Apple Intelligence integration, Anthropic expanded its Amazon partnership, and Google paid out new revenue-share terms to Samsung to keep Gemini as the default Galaxy assistant. Perplexity itself had been on an aggressive partnership tear — landing carrier-level distribution with T-Mobile, an integration with Motorola devices, and a high-profile (and controversial) $34.5 billion unsolicited bid for Google Chrome in August 2025.

The pattern across the sector has been clear: foundation-model and answer-engine companies are willing to pay surface owners enormous sums for default placement, because consumer AI economics are dictated by who shows up first when a user has a question. But several of these arrangements — including reported friction inside the Apple-OpenAI partnership and the slow rollout of various OEM AI features — have shown that big upfront payments don't guarantee the integration actually ships at quality, on time, or with aligned incentives.

Perplexity's reported 2025 ARR was in the $150M range against a $9B+ valuation, meaning a $400M outflow over 12 months would have been an aggressive customer-acquisition bet even by hyperscale-AI standards. The math may simply have stopped working for one or both sides.

What To Do Next

If you're a developer building on Perplexity's API: nothing changes today. The Sonar API, Comet browser integrations, and enterprise endpoints are unaffected. But factor in that Perplexity's consumer reach just got smaller — if your product depends on Perplexity becoming a default consumer surface, re-baseline your distribution assumptions.

If you're a buyer evaluating AI search vendors: use this as leverage. Perplexity now has an unspent distribution budget and a need to demonstrate enterprise traction. Pricing on multi-seat and API contracts is likely to soften over the next two quarters.

If you're a Snapchat user: the My AI experience you have today doesn't change. The Perplexity-branded answer experience that was previewed in late 2025 is shelved.

If you're tracking the sector: watch three things over the next 90 days — (1) whether Snap announces a replacement AI partner, (2) whether Perplexity's next funding round gets repriced, and (3) whether any portion of the $400M shows up as a one-time item in Snap's next earnings.

💡
For founders negotiating distribution deals: structure payments against shipped milestones, not calendar dates. The Snap–Perplexity unwind is the cleanest argument yet for tying every tranche to a measurable integration KPI — DAU on the feature, query volume, retention — rather than a flat 12-month schedule.
