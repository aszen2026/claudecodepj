What Happened

An independent developer posted a free, full-length "Build Your Own LLM" workshop to YouTube that walks through rebuilding OpenAI's GPT-2-style Transformer from scratch — and the announcement landed on Reddit today, June 6, 2026, where it climbed the machine-learning subreddits. The pitch is simple and increasingly common: not "fine-tune an API," but "implement the whole thing yourself," from tokenizer and attention blocks to the training loop.

The model at the center of it is not new. OpenAI shipped GPT-2 in February 2019, and the story around it was almost as famous as the model itself: the company initially declined to release the full 1.5-billion-parameter weights, calling them too risky to publish, before releasing all four sizes — 124M, 355M, 774M, and 1.5B parameters — by November 2019. What is news is the role GPT-2 now plays. The 2019 "frontier" model has become the default classroom specimen for an entire genre of teaching content.

The headline numbers that make that genre possible:

  • Andrej Karpathy's open-source llm.c project reproduced GPT-2 124M for roughly $20 of cloud GPU time in about 90 minutes on a single 8×A100 node.
  • The same project reproduced the full 1.5B-class model for roughly $672 over about a day on that same node.
  • By contrast, Sam Altman has said training GPT-4 cost north of $100 million, and GPT-3 has been estimated at around $4.6 million.

A model that was treated as dangerous to release in 2019 is now a reproducible weekend exercise — and this YouTube workshop is the latest entry in a now-crowded shelf.

Why It Matters

The significance is not the model — it is the collapse in the cost of understanding one. For most of the post-ChatGPT era, the dominant mental model has been that large language models are black-box magic that only well-funded labs can build. A workshop that rebuilds GPT-2 line by line directly attacks that assumption.

Who wins: students, self-taught engineers, bootcamps, and the broader open-source community. Decoder-only Transformers — the GPT-2 architecture — are the shared ancestor of essentially every modern model, including Llama, Mistral, Qwen, and DeepSeek. Learn GPT-2's internals and you have read the blueprint for the entire field, not a toy.

Who loses, in a narrow sense, is the mystique premium. Teams that have marketed themselves on "we trained our own LLM" lose some of that aura when the architectural core is a documented YouTube tutorial. The real moat was never the model code; it is data, scale, alignment, and inference infrastructure — and this kind of content makes that distinction obvious to a much wider audience.

💡
The gap that survives: Rebuilding GPT-2 teaches you the architecture, not the product. The distance between a from-scratch GPT-2 and a deployable assistant is overwhelmingly data quality, training scale, RLHF/preference tuning, and serving infrastructure — almost none of which is "the Transformer." Treat the architecture as the easy 10%.

Context

This workshop sits in a well-established lineage. Karpathy's nanoGPT and his "Let's reproduce GPT-2" video turned the model into a canonical teaching target; his llm.c project pushed the same reproduction into raw C/CUDA. Sebastian Raschka's book Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch) did it for the print-and-Jupyter crowd. Hugging Face's tutorials and the minGPT family round out the shelf. The new YouTube workshop is notable mainly as evidence that the format has gone fully mainstream — it is now expected, not novel, that a "build your own LLM" course exists for any learning style.

It also fits the broader open-weights wave. Since 2023, Meta's Llama line, Mistral, Alibaba's Qwen, and DeepSeek have normalized publishing capable model weights. GPT-2 is the on-ramp to that ecosystem: small enough to train on a hobbyist budget, architecturally honest about how the big models work, and unencumbered enough to teach with freely. The 2019 "too dangerous to release" framing reads, in 2026, as a historical curiosity — a useful reminder of how fast the perceived risk and the actual accessibility of these systems have moved.

What To Do Next

For learners: Start at the bottom of the size ladder. Build and train the 124M model first — it is the cheapest path to a working end-to-end pipeline, and tools like nanoGPT and llm.c let you do it for tens of dollars rather than thousands. Pair a video workshop like this one with a written reference (Raschka's book or the nanoGPT repo) so you have both the spoken walkthrough and code you can pause on.

💡
Budget reality check: A 124M reproduction is roughly a $20 cloud run; the 1.5B class is a few hundred dollars. If a course implies you'll train something "GPT-4-class" at home, it is selling the wrong expectation — those runs are eight-figure efforts.

For developers and teams: Use this as a literacy exercise, not a product plan. Understanding GPT-2 internals makes you a sharper consumer of the APIs and open weights you actually ship on — better at debugging tokenization, context limits, and sampling behavior. But for production, stand on existing open-weights models rather than training from zero.

For buyers and managers: Recalibrate what "we build LLMs" should cost you. Architecture is now a teachable commodity; vet vendors and hires on their data pipelines, evaluation discipline, and inference economics instead. And when interviewing engineers, a candidate who has personally rebuilt GPT-2 is demonstrating exactly the kind of from-first-principles understanding that separates operators from prompt-pasters.
