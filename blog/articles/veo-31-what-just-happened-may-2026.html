Veo 3.1: What Just Happened (May 2026)

We tested the leading veo 3.1 tools and broke down exactly what works, what doesn't, and which option fits your workflow. No sponsored opinions — just honest analysis.

AIRankHub Editorial Independent AI Research · May 13, 2026
🤖

What Happened

Google DeepMind quietly pushed Veo 3.1 into general availability this week, and the most immediate noise around the release isn't coming from Google's own blog — it's coming from the creator community. A workflow circulating under the name NanoBanana 2 has demonstrated a reliable technique for generating perfectly square (1:1 aspect ratio) video outputs from Veo 3.1, a format the model's official interface does not natively expose.

The core discovery: Veo 3.1 retains enough internal flexibility in its spatial reasoning that carefully structured prompts — plus a lightweight post-processing pass baked into the NanoBanana 2 pipeline — can coerce the model into producing clean 1080×1080 outputs without visible cropping artifacts. The resulting videos hold subject framing and motion coherence well enough for social-first content pipelines.

Veo 3.1 itself is an incremental update to Veo 3, which launched in May 2025 as Google's first video model with native synchronized audio generation. The 3.1 revision sharpens temporal consistency — specifically reducing the "drift" problem where subjects gradually deform over clips longer than eight seconds — and extends maximum output length from 60 to 90 seconds at 1080p.

💡
Quick spec check: Veo 3.1 is accessible via Google's VideoFX consumer interface and through the Vertex AI API (model ID veo-3.1). Vertex pricing remains at $0.35 per second of generated video. The NanoBanana 2 workflow is open-source and runs locally; it wraps the Vertex AI endpoint and requires a GCP project with the Video AI API enabled.

Why It Matters

The square-format hack is more than a curiosity. Instagram Reels, TikTok profile grids, and YouTube Shorts thumbnails all favor 1:1 or 9:16 crops, and the absence of native square output from Veo 3.1 would have pushed social media teams back toward manual crop-and-reframe workflows. NanoBanana 2 closes that gap today, without waiting for Google to ship an official aspect ratio toggle.

This matters competitively because Sora (OpenAI) and Runway Gen-4 both offer multi-ratio output as a first-class feature. If Veo 3.1 requires a community workaround to match that capability, it signals a product prioritization gap Google will need to address — likely in a future Veo 3.x patch or a Veo 4 launch later this year.

For enterprise buyers, the workaround carries a practical warning: NanoBanana 2 routes outputs through a local ffmpeg pass before returning results, which means generated video technically leaves the Vertex AI trust boundary before landing in a production system. For teams under strict data residency requirements, that's a blocker, not a feature.

Who wins immediately: indie creators and small agencies who can move fast on open-source tooling and don't have compliance overhead. Who waits: regulated industries, studios with IP sensitivity, and any team that needs SLA-backed support for their video pipeline.

Context

Veo has followed an unusually fast release cadence. Veo 1 landed in mid-2024 as a research preview, Veo 2 shipped commercially in late 2024 with improved physics simulation, and Veo 3 in May 2025 was the first model to add audio natively — a capability Runway and Sora had to bolt on separately. Veo 3.1 continuing that cadence with a focused stability patch is consistent with DeepMind treating video generation as a priority product line, not a research experiment.

The NanoBanana workflow itself is part of a broader pattern of the open-source community outpacing official product UIs. Similar gap-filling tools emerged around Stable Video Diffusion's aspect ratio limitations in 2024 and around Sora's early access restrictions. The community reliably finds ways to extract capabilities the vendor hasn't formally productized yet.

💡
Competitive landscape note: Runway Gen-4 currently supports 16:9, 9:16, and 1:1 natively at launch. Sora supports custom aspect ratios via the API. Veo 3.1's lack of native square output is now the most visible functional gap versus direct competitors in the social-content segment.

Google's VideoFX consumer wrapper is also still US-only in its full feature set, which limits Veo 3.1 adoption outside the Vertex AI enterprise path in most markets. NanoBanana 2's Vertex-first approach inadvertently makes the model more globally accessible for developers who already have GCP billing set up.

What To Do Next

  • Developers building social content pipelines: Evaluate NanoBanana 2 now if 1:1 output is a hard requirement. Read the project's data handling documentation before connecting it to any pipeline that processes client assets — confirm the local ffmpeg step is acceptable under your data agreements.
  • Vertex AI users already on Veo 3: The model ID change to veo-3.1 is required; the 3.0 endpoint is not auto-upgraded. Update your API calls and run regression tests on clips longer than 30 seconds where the temporal consistency improvements will be most visible.
  • Teams evaluating video AI vendors: Add native aspect ratio support and maximum output duration to your scoring rubric. Veo 3.1's 90-second ceiling is now the longest available at 1080p among the major commercial models — worth weighting if long-form content is in scope.
  • Compliance-sensitive organizations: Wait for Google to ship official 1:1 support rather than routing through community tools. Given the cadence of Veo point releases, that's likely a matter of weeks to months, not a year.

The NanoBanana 2 workflow is available on GitHub. Veo 3.1 is live in Vertex AI today. Native square output from Google remains, for now, a feature request.
