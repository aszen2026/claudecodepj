What Happened

In an interview published July 9, 2026, Nigeria winger Moses Simon revealed that a collapsed move to Liverpool is the biggest regret of his career. The 30-year-old, who has earned close to 98 caps for the Super Eagles, was speaking shortly after joining Ligue 1 side Paris FC following his exit from FC Nantes.

The story dates back to his spell at Belgian club KAA Gent. Between 2016 and 2018, Simon says a reported £10 million transfer to Anfield came closest to happening. It never did. Liverpool instead signed Xherdan Shaqiri in the summer of 2018, and Simon stayed put.

The key details he disclosed:

  • The suitors: He claims Liverpool, Tottenham, Brighton, Fulham, Hull City and Lyon all tracked him during his Gent years.
  • The self-inflicted wound: Simon admits he deliberately let his form drop, believing poor performances would pressure Gent into selling him.
  • The price problem: Gent had signed him for around €1 million and, sensing the interest, held out for closer to €25 million — pricing the deal out of reach.
  • His verdict: "I was supposed to go to Liverpool. It didn't work." He now calls easing off "an important lesson."

Why It Matters

This is less a transfer-window bombshell than a rare, candid post-mortem from inside a failed deal — and that candor is what gives it weight.

Who "loses" in hindsight: Simon himself is the clearest casualty. A move to a Jürgen Klopp side entering its peak years — Liverpool won the Champions League in 2019 and the Premier League in 2020 — would have altered his career trajectory. Instead he spent five seasons at Nantes and is now at a newly ambitious but unproven Paris FC.

Who "wins": Gent, who protected their asset and their valuation, and arguably Liverpool, whose recruitment team read the situation and pivoted to Shaqiri rather than overpay for a player actively downing tools.

💡
The real lesson isn't about Liverpool. It's about leverage. Simon assumed a dip in output would force a sale. In reality it did the opposite: it handed the selling club a reason to hold firm and priced him out of his dream move. Underperformance is never a negotiating tactic.

Context

Simon's admission lands in a well-worn genre: the veteran player reflecting on the transfer that got away. What makes his version useful is the mechanism he describes.

The 2016–2018 window was a period when the analytics-driven wave of recruitment — the same era that produced Liverpool's data-heavy signings under Michael Edwards — was hardening clubs against emotional or pressure-based deals. A player trying to engineer an exit by sulking was exactly the profile those systems were built to screen out.

It also fits a pattern specific to the Belgian league, which for a decade has functioned as a selling market: buy cheap, develop, and demand a premium. Gent turning a ~€1m purchase into a €25m ask is the model working as designed. Simon was caught between his ambition and his club's business logic, and the business logic won.

His subsequent path — Levante, then Nantes, and now Paris FC — kept him in strong leagues without ever reaching the Champions League-regular tier Liverpool represented. That gap is the quiet subtext of the whole interview.

What To Do Next

For different readers, the takeaways diverge:

  • Young players: Simon's own advice is the headline — "the more you play, the more market value you have." Keep performing even when a move stalls. As he put it, if the transfer comes, you go; if it doesn't, "continue, because your club is paying you."
  • Agents and advisors: Manage the mindset, not just the paperwork. The damage here was psychological — speculation eroded focus before it eroded form. Build in a plan for the window not closing.
  • Clubs and scouts: Treat a sudden, unexplained form dip in a linked player as a data point, not a discount. Liverpool's decision to walk and sign Shaqiri has aged well.
  • Fans following Paris FC: Read this as motivation, not baggage. A player who has publicly named his biggest regret is one with a point to prove at a club still building its identity.
💡
Watch the summer window. With Simon settling at Paris FC and speaking this openly at 30, expect his form and any renewed interest to be framed through this narrative. The interview didn't just reveal a regret — it set the storyline for the next chapter of his career.
