Google Launch: What Just Happened (May 2026)

What Happened

On May 7, 2026, Google pushed its workplace AI battle into a new gear with a coordinated launch aimed squarely at Microsoft Copilot and the freshly announced Slackbot AI agent from Salesforce. The release bundles an upgraded Gemini Enterprise agent layer into Workspace, deepens Agentspace integrations across Gmail, Docs, Meet, and Drive, and opens a developer surface so third parties can register agents that run inside the Workspace shell rather than as external chat apps.

The headline pieces from today's announcement:

  • Workspace-native agents that can read context across a user's mailbox, calendar, and Drive without per-app prompting, gated behind admin-controlled data boundaries.
  • Agent Gallery in Workspace, a directory where IT admins can publish internal agents and approve third-party ones — Google's answer to Microsoft's Copilot Studio agent store.
  • Pricing repositioned around Gemini Enterprise tiers rather than the older Gemini for Workspace add-on SKUs, folding agent usage into the seat price for Business and Enterprise customers.
  • Meet and Chat upgrades, including agents that can join meetings as participants, take structured action items, and trigger downstream tasks in Drive or third-party SaaS via the new agent connectors.

The launch lands the same week Salesforce rolled out its Slackbot AI agent, which positions Slack as the conversational front door for Agentforce. Microsoft, meanwhile, continues to ship Copilot agent updates inside Microsoft 365. Three of the largest distribution channels in enterprise software are now actively shipping agent platforms in the same quarter.

Why It Matters

The competitive frame has shifted from "which assistant writes the best email" to "which platform owns the agent runtime inside the workday." Whoever controls that runtime collects the data flows, the audit logs, the billing relationships, and — most importantly — the default integration surface that ISVs build against.

Google's bet is that Gmail plus Drive plus Calendar is a stickier substrate for agents than Slack channels or Teams threads, because most knowledge work still terminates in a document or an inbox. By making agents a Workspace-native primitive instead of a bolt-on, Google is trying to turn its existing 3-billion-user install base into the default surface for agent invocations rather than ceding that role to Slack or Teams.

Likely winners: Workspace-heavy customers who were paying for ChatGPT Enterprise or Copilot on top of Google licenses — they get a credible single-vendor option. ISVs already in the Google Workspace Marketplace get a faster path to agent distribution. Likely losers: standalone "AI assistant" startups whose value proposition was a thin wrapper over Gmail, and Copilot Studio resellers whose differentiation just narrowed.

💡
The real KPI to watch: not feature parity with Copilot, but how many agents actually get published to the Agent Gallery in the next 90 days. A platform without ISV gravity is just a chatbot with extra steps.

Context

Today's launch is the third leg of a strategy that has been visible for roughly 18 months. Google introduced Agentspace as a standalone enterprise search and agent product, then began stitching it into Workspace SKUs late last year. Gemini's model line has, in parallel, moved toward longer context windows and tool-use reliability — the prerequisites for agents that don't fail silently mid-task.

The competitive backdrop is unusually crowded for a single week:

  • Salesforce shipped the Slackbot AI agent, extending Agentforce into the Slack surface and pitching Slack as the connective tissue across Salesforce, Box, Google Drive, and Microsoft 365.
  • Microsoft continues iterating on Copilot agents inside Teams and the Microsoft 365 app, with Copilot Studio as the authoring layer.
  • OpenAI and Anthropic have both pushed deeper into the enterprise with Claude for Work and ChatGPT Enterprise, but neither owns the productivity suite — they sit on top of one.

The broader trend: the enterprise AI conversation has moved off the model layer and onto the distribution layer. Differentiation now comes from where the agent lives, what it can see, and which admin console governs it.

What To Do Next

For IT and procurement teams already on Workspace, the immediate task is unglamorous but high-leverage: re-baseline AI spend now that agent usage is bundled into Gemini Enterprise tiers. Many orgs are double-paying for Workspace plus a third-party assistant, and today's pricing change makes that harder to justify at renewal.

For developers and ISVs, the Agent Gallery is worth treating as a first-class distribution channel rather than a checkbox. Early entrants on the Microsoft Copilot agent store captured outsized installs in the first two quarters; the same window is likely opening here.

For platform-neutral buyers evaluating across Google, Microsoft, and Salesforce, the practical comparison points are concrete:

  • Data boundary controls — can you scope an agent to a specific Drive folder, Slack channel, or SharePoint site, with auditable logs?
  • Identity and DLP integration — does the agent inherit your existing SSO, conditional access, and data-loss-prevention policies, or does it create a parallel permission model?
  • Failure semantics — what does the agent do when a tool call fails or returns ambiguous data? Silent skips and hallucinated successes are the most common cause of pulled pilots.
  • Exit cost — if you build 50 internal agents in one vendor's authoring tool, how portable are they?
💡
For developers: register at least a stub agent in each of the three galleries (Google Agent Gallery, Microsoft Copilot Studio, Salesforce AppExchange) before committing to one. The cost is a weekend; the optionality is a year of distribution.
