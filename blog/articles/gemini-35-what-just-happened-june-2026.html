Gemini 3.5: What Just Happened (June 2026)

We tested the leading gemini 3.5 tools and broke down exactly what works, what doesn't, and which option fits your workflow. No sponsored opinions — just honest analysis.

A
AIRankHub Editorial Independent AI Research · June 09, 2026
🤖

What Happened

On June 9, 2026, Google began rolling out a Gemini 3.5 upgrade to NotebookLM, its AI research-and-notetaking app. The update does more than swap in a smarter model. Google is bundling two new capabilities into NotebookLM: a cloud computer — an agentic environment that can browse, run tasks, and operate tools on the user's behalf — and a source-finding assistant that helps users locate and pull in relevant documents instead of requiring them to upload everything manually.

The same release brings Antigravity, Google's agentic coding-and-automation surface, into NotebookLM as well. Google describes the rollout as happening "across the board," but with a notable catch on access:

  • What's new: Gemini 3.5 as the underlying model, a cloud computer for agentic actions, automated source discovery, and Antigravity integration.
  • Who gets it first: the upgrade is gated to Google AI Ultra subscribers (and the higher paid tiers), not the free NotebookLM audience.
  • When: rollout started June 9, 2026, on a phased basis.

In short: NotebookLM is graduating from a passive "chat with your documents" tool into an agent that can go find the documents and act on them.

💡
The key shift: Earlier NotebookLM answered questions about sources you supplied. With Gemini 3.5 and a cloud computer, it can now go fetch the sources and take steps on them. That's the difference between a librarian and a research assistant.

Why It Matters

NotebookLM has been one of Google's most-praised AI products precisely because it stayed grounded — it answered only from the sources you gave it, which kept hallucinations low. Adding a cloud computer and automated source-finding changes that contract. The product is now reaching outward, which makes it far more useful for real research workflows but also raises the stakes on accuracy and provenance.

Who wins:

  • Heavy researchers, analysts, and students who previously spent hours gathering PDFs, articles, and reports before NotebookLM could help. The source-finder collapses that step.
  • Google's AI Ultra subscription business. By reserving Gemini 3.5, the cloud computer, and Antigravity for paid Ultra tiers, Google gives power users a concrete reason to pay up rather than treating NotebookLM as a free novelty.
  • Developers who get Antigravity inside a tool they already use for research, blurring the line between gathering knowledge and building on it.

Who loses, or at least waits:

  • Free-tier users stay on the older experience for now. The most interesting capabilities are behind a paywall.
  • Standalone research-agent startups that pitched "find sources and reason over them" as their whole product. Google is now shipping that natively to a large installed base.

Context

This release fits a clear 2025–2026 pattern: AI vendors are racing to turn chat assistants into agents that can use a computer, browse, and complete multi-step tasks rather than just generate text. Google folding a "cloud computer" into a mainstream consumer product like NotebookLM is the consumer-facing version of that bet.

It also reflects how Google is positioning its model lineup. Gemini 3.5 is the engine, but Google is increasingly selling experiences built on top of it — NotebookLM, Antigravity, and the AI Ultra subscription — rather than just raw model access. Tying the newest model to the top paid tier mirrors how rivals stage their best capabilities behind premium plans.

The competitive backdrop is direct. The major labs have all pushed agentic, tool-using assistants and document-grounded research products. By upgrading NotebookLM — a product with genuine word-of-mouth loyalty — Google is defending one of the few AI tools where it has a clear lead in user affection, and trying to convert that goodwill into recurring revenue.

💡
Watch the source citations. NotebookLM's reputation rests on grounded, citable answers. As it starts fetching its own sources, judge the upgrade on whether every claim still traces back to a verifiable document — not just on how much faster it gathers them.

What To Do Next

If you're an existing NotebookLM user: check whether your account is on Google AI Ultra. The cloud computer, source-finder, Gemini 3.5, and Antigravity are gated to that tier at launch, so a free or lower-paid account won't see them yet. If the workflow is core to your job, the Ultra plan is now the deciding factor.

If you're evaluating it for research or knowledge work: run a side-by-side test. Give the new source-finder a topic and verify that the documents it pulls are authoritative and correctly cited — don't assume auto-gathered sources are as trustworthy as ones you vetted yourself. Spot-check provenance before you rely on a notebook for anything that ships.

If you're a developer: explore the Antigravity integration as a way to move from research to action inside one tool. Treat the cloud computer as an agent with real reach — scope what it's allowed to touch, and review its steps before trusting it with sensitive accounts or data.

If you're a buyer comparing AI suites: price the decision around AI Ultra, not NotebookLM in isolation. The value calculation now includes Gemini 3.5, the agentic cloud computer, and Antigravity as a bundle. Compare that against rival premium plans on the tasks your team actually performs, not on feature checklists.
