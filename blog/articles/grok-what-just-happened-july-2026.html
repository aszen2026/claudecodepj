grok: What Just Happened (July 2026)

We tested the leading grok tools and broke down exactly what works, what doesn't, and which option fits your workflow. No sponsored opinions — just honest analysis.

A
AIRankHub Editorial Independent AI Research · July 17, 2026
🤖
I have enough corroborated detail. The story is Grok 4.5's July 8 launch, priced aggressively against a very different competitive backdrop than xAI's last release — the company is now part of a public SpaceX.

What Happened

On Wednesday, July 8, 2026, SpaceXAI released Grok 4.5, its first frontier model since SpaceX went public. Elon Musk described it as "an Opus-class model, but faster, more token-efficient and lower cost." The model is available now in Grok Build, in Cursor across all plans, and through the SpaceXAI console.

The headline number is price. Grok 4.5 costs $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens, with configurable reasoning effort (low, medium, high; default high) and serving speeds around 80 tokens per second. That undercuts the field substantially:

  • Grok 4.5 — $2 in / $6 out
  • Claude Opus 4.8 — $5 in / $25 out
  • GPT-5.5 / 5.6 — $5 in / $30 out
  • Claude Fable 5 — $10 in / $50 out

On xAI's own published benchmarks, Grok 4.5 is competitive but not best-in-class. It scored 83.3% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, effectively tied with GPT-5.5 at 83.4% and just behind Fable 5 at 84.3%. On harder agentic coding work the gaps widen: 64.7% on SWE-Bench Pro versus Opus 4.8's 69.2% and Fable 5's 80.4%, and 53% on DeepSWE 1.1 against GPT-5.5's 67% and Fable 5's 70%.

The more interesting claim is efficiency. xAI reports Grok 4.5 resolves SWE-Bench Pro tasks using an average of 15,954 output tokens, against roughly 67,020 for Opus 4.8 — a 4.2× gap — and about 1.9M total tokens per task versus 6.2M for GPT-5.5 and 7.2M for Fable 5. The model was trained alongside Cursor, whose parent company SpaceX agreed to buy in June.

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 the following day, meaning xAI's comparison charts were obsolete within roughly 24 hours.

💡
Read the price comparison as a per-task number, not a per-token one. If Grok 4.5 uses ~4× fewer output tokens than Opus 4.8 and charges ~4× less per token, the compounded cost difference on an agentic coding job is closer to an order of magnitude than the sticker prices suggest. Whether that holds on your workload is an empirical question — measure it.

Why It Matters

This release marks the point where frontier-model competition stopped being purely about capability scores and started being about cost per completed task. Grok 4.5 loses several benchmarks it published — and xAI shipped anyway, because the pitch is that a model which is 90% as good and 5–10× cheaper wins most real budgets.

Who wins: Cursor's users, who get a frontier-adjacent model bundled across all plans. High-volume agentic workloads, where token spend, not raw capability, is the binding constraint. SpaceXAI itself, which now has both a model and one of the largest distribution channels in AI coding under one roof.

Who's squeezed: Anthropic and OpenAI at the mid-tier. Fable 5 at $10/$50 still leads on the hardest coding work by a wide margin (80.4% vs 64.7% on SWE-Bench Pro), and that gap justifies its price for genuinely hard problems. But the routine 70% of agentic work has just been repriced underneath both companies.

One caveat deserves weight. Artificial Analysis places Grok 4.5 fourth on its Intelligence Index — behind Fable 5, GPT-5.5, and Opus 4.8 — and measured its hallucination rate at 54%, up from 25% in the prior version. If that holds up, the efficiency win is partly a reliability trade: fewer tokens spent, more verification required.

Context

Grok 4.5 is the first model shipped by a fundamentally different company than the one that shipped Grok 4. The corporate sequence over six months:

  • February 2, 2026 — SpaceX absorbs xAI in an all-stock merger valuing the combined entity at $1.25 trillion ($1T SpaceX, $250B xAI), folding in Grok and the X platform. Musk framed it around vertically integrating AI with orbital data centers.
  • June 11, 2026 — SpaceX raises $75 billion in the largest IPO on record, pricing 555,555,555 shares at $135 under the ticker SPCX.
  • June 16, 2026 — SpaceX agrees to acquire Anysphere, Cursor's parent, for $60 billion in stock — the largest acquisition of a venture-backed startup ever. Cursor reached roughly $4B annualized revenue in under four years; the deal is expected to close in Q3 2026, pending regulatory approval.

That context explains the pricing. A newly public company with a $60B coding acquisition closing needs Cursor's model economics to work, and $2/$6 with 4× token efficiency is what "works" looks like on a gross-margin slide.

On the consumer side, Musk declared Grok Imagine "done" on July 5, then shipped another Imagine update on July 7 — a reminder that "done" is a mood, not a roadmap.

What To Do Next

If you're a Cursor user: Grok 4.5 is already available on your plan. Try it on mid-difficulty tasks first — refactors, test generation, well-scoped features — where the benchmark gap is narrow and the cost gap is wide. Keep Opus or Fable for the gnarly debugging sessions.

If you're a developer choosing an API: Don't benchmark on price per token. Run your actual task suite and measure total cost per completed task, plus a rework rate. Given the 54% hallucination figure, add a verification step and count its cost too. The reasoning-effort dial (low/medium/high) is real leverage — default high is not always correct.

If you're a buyer: Use this release as pricing leverage now. Two frontier labs just had their mid-tier undercut by 60–80%, and enterprise contracts signed before July at Opus/GPT list prices are worth reopening.

💡
Every benchmark in xAI's launch materials compares against GPT-5.5, not GPT-5.6 — which shipped a day later. Treat the published charts as a snapshot with a 24-hour shelf life, and wait for independent evaluations before making a migration decision.
