grok: What Just Happened (July 2026)

SpaceXAI shipped Grok 4.5 on July 8, 2026, and Elon Musk called it "an Opus-class model, but faster, more token-efficient and lower cost." Nine days later, the independent numbers tell a more complicated story: the model is a genuine efficiency win and a fourth-place finisher, at the same time.

What Happened

Grok 4.5 is the first model release from SpaceXAI since two structural events reshaped the company. SpaceX — which had already merged with xAI earlier this year — completed the largest IPO in history, raising roughly $86 billion at a $1.7 trillion valuation. The stock ran from its $135 IPO price to north of $200 within days, pushing the valuation past $2.7 trillion. On June 16, the company used that inflated stock as currency to acquire AI coding startup Cursor (Anysphere) for $60 billion in stock — the largest startup acquisition ever recorded, and roughly the price of a company that had been days away from closing a $2 billion round at a $50 billion valuation from Andreessen Horowitz, Thrive, and Nvidia. The deal is expected to close in Q3.

Grok 4.5 is what that acquisition bought. Reported at roughly 1.5 trillion parameters, it was trained alongside Cursor and aimed squarely at coding, agentic tasks, and knowledge work. The specifics:

  • Pricing: $2 per 1M input tokens, $0.50 cached input, $6 per 1M output — with higher rates above 200K tokens.
  • Context window: 500,000 tokens — notably a reduction from Grok 4.3's 1M.
  • Speed: ~80 tokens/second, with configurable reasoning effort (low/medium/high, default high).
  • Availability: Grok Build, Cursor on all plans, and the SpaceXAI console.

The benchmarks SpaceXAI chose to publish are split. Grok 4.5 beats Claude Opus 4.8 on DeepSWE 1.0 (62.0% vs 55.75%) and Terminal Bench 2.1 (83.3% vs 78.9%). It loses on the neutral-harness DeepSWE 1.1 run (53% vs 59%) and on SWE Bench Pro (64.7% vs 69.2%). Anthropic's Fable 5 leads all four rows the company selected. Artificial Analysis — the benchmarking firm engineering teams increasingly treat as a neutral referee — placed Grok 4.5 fourth on its Intelligence Index at 54 points, behind Fable 5 (60), Opus 4.8 (56), and GPT-5.5 (55). That is fourth place on a leaderboard where Musk had claimed a tie for first. The same testing reportedly found a hallucination rate that more than doubled, to 54% — a reliability figure absent from the launch materials.

The efficiency claim, though, holds up and is the real headline. On SWE Bench Pro, Grok 4.5 averages 15,954 output tokens per task versus 67,020 for Opus 4.8 at max effort — about 4.2x fewer.

💡
Read the harness, not just the score. Grok 4.5 wins DeepSWE 1.0 on the provider's own harness and loses DeepSWE 1.1 on the neutral one. When a model's ranking flips based on who ran the eval, the harness is doing real work in the result. Always ask which one produced the number.

Why It Matters

Musk benchmarked against Opus 4.7, not the shipping 4.8 — a comparison against a model one version stale. That's the tell for what this release actually is: not a frontier-capability claim that survived contact with independent testing, but a cost-per-task claim that did.

And cost-per-task is the axis that matters most for agentic work. An agent loop burning 67,000 output tokens per task at Opus pricing versus 16,000 at $6/1M is a materially different bill, repeated thousands of times a day. Grok 4.5 doesn't have to be the smartest model to win budget — it has to be good enough at a fraction of the spend. That's a real threat to Anthropic and OpenAI at the mid-tier of the market, and much less of one at the top, where teams pay for the last few points of capability regardless.

The losers are clearer than the winners. Independent coding-tool startups now compete with a Cursor backed by a $2.7 trillion balance sheet and a vertically integrated model. And the 54% hallucination figure, if it holds, prices this model out of exactly the finance and legal applications the launch positioned it for.

Context

Grok 4.5 is not Grok 5, and that gap is the story SpaceXAI would rather you didn't notice. Grok 5 — the leading candidate being a 6-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model training on Colossus 2 — has now slipped through both its Q1 and Q2 2026 windows, with Q3 or later the current expectation. What shipped instead was a run of adjacent releases: Grok Voice (June 4), Grok Imagine Video 1.5 topping the image-to-video leaderboard, Musk declaring Grok Imagine "done" on July 5, and Grok 4.5 on July 8. Productive, but lateral.

The shrinking context window — 1M down to 500K — points the same direction. This is a model tuned for throughput economics, not a capability leap. Meanwhile, on July 15, SpaceXAI open-sourced Grok Build on GitHub with a local-first setup and inspectable agent loops. That's a distribution play, not a model play: get the harness everywhere, and the model follows.

What To Do Next

Concrete guidance depending on where you sit:

  • Evaluate on your own tasks, in your own harness. The DeepSWE 1.0/1.1 split is proof that published rankings won't transfer cleanly. Run your real workload.
  • Measure cost per completed task, not per token. A $6/1M model that finishes in 16K tokens beats a cheaper model that flails for 60K. This is the metric Grok 4.5 was built to win — and the one most teams aren't tracking.
  • Test hallucination rates before anything regulated. Given the 54% figure, put Grok 4.5 behind verification for finance, legal, or medical outputs, or keep it out of them.
  • Check the 200K pricing cliff. Rates rise above 200K tokens and the ceiling is 500K. If you were relying on Grok 4.3's 1M window, this is a migration, not an upgrade.
  • Cursor customers: don't re-architect yet. The acquisition hasn't closed. Keep your model layer swappable.
💡
The cheapest benchmark is the one you already own. Take 20 tasks your team actually shipped last month, replay them against Grok 4.5, Opus 4.8, and GPT-5.5, and log tokens, wall-clock time, and pass rate. That afternoon of work is worth more than every leaderboard in this article.
