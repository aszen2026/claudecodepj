Nvidia $6B Deal: What Just Happened (May 2026)

What Happened

Snowflake has signed a $6 billion multi-year deal with Amazon Web Services for AI compute capacity, with the agreement explicitly favoring AWS's in-house silicon over the Nvidia GPUs that have dominated AI infrastructure spend for the past three years. The reference reporting frames this as a CPU chip deal — pointing to AWS's Graviton line for general AI serving workloads alongside the Trainium AI accelerator family for heavier training and inference.

Key facts from the announcement:

  • Contract value: $6B, structured as a multi-year commitment rather than a single capex outlay.
  • Counterparties: Snowflake (the data-cloud vendor) and AWS (its founding infrastructure partner).
  • Workload target: AI compute for Snowflake's Cortex AI and agentic services running inside customer accounts.
  • Notable omission: Nvidia is not named as a chip supplier in this deal — a striking absence given Nvidia's roughly 80% share of merchant AI silicon revenue heading into 2026.

Why It Matters

This is the largest publicly disclosed commitment to non-Nvidia AI silicon by a major SaaS platform vendor to date, and it reshapes three conversations at once.

The Winners

  • AWS: Validates a half-decade of custom-silicon investment. Trainium and Graviton are no longer just internal cost-savers — they now anchor an external customer contract at a scale that justifies the next generation of tape-outs.
  • Snowflake: Locks in unit economics for Cortex AI. If AWS silicon delivers the price-performance AWS has been claiming (roughly 30–40% better $/token versus comparable GPU instances in their public benchmarks), Snowflake captures margin its competitors running on H100/H200 cannot match.
  • Amazon (the parent): A second consecutive quarter of "good news" — pairing the recently disclosed Anthropic Trainium expansion with a new external anchor tenant.

The Losers

  • Nvidia: Not a catastrophe — but a clear signal that the duopoly-customer concentration (hyperscalers + a few labs) carries directional risk. Snowflake was a plausible $6B Nvidia customer; it just chose otherwise.
  • Pure-play GPU clouds: CoreWeave, Lambda, and Crusoe sell Nvidia capacity. A $6B deal that bypasses them entirely narrows their addressable market.
💡
The deal is denominated in compute capacity, not silicon units. That matters: Snowflake is buying outcomes (tokens served, models trained), letting AWS optimize the chip mix behind the scenes. Expect AWS to lean increasingly on Trainium2/Trainium3 as that hardware ships.

Context

This contract slots into a pattern that has accelerated through 2025 and into 2026:

  • Anthropic's Project Rainier (announced late 2024, scaled through 2025) committed Anthropic to hundreds of thousands of Trainium2 chips — the proof point AWS needed for frontier-scale training on its own silicon.
  • Google's TPU v6 (Trillium) and the v7 successor have been winning external workloads from Apple, Salesforce, and others — establishing that "not Nvidia" is now a credible enterprise choice, not just an internal cost optimization.
  • Microsoft's Maia 200 entered broader internal deployment in early 2026 for OpenAI inference, completing the hyperscaler trifecta of credible in-house accelerators.
  • Snowflake's prior posture was hardware-agnostic and Nvidia-friendly — its 2024 partnerships included Nvidia NIM integration. A $6B AWS-silicon deal represents a directional shift, not a continuation.

The macro story: hyperscaler custom silicon has moved from "cost optimization for internal workloads" to "competitive weapon for landing external AI contracts." Snowflake is the first marquee non-AI-lab customer to ratify that thesis at scale.

What To Do Next

For developers building on Snowflake

  • Expect Cortex AI pricing to become more aggressive over the next 2–4 quarters as Snowflake passes through silicon savings. Re-evaluate workloads that were uneconomic at GPU prices.
  • Get familiar with the AWS Neuron SDK if you write low-level inference code — Snowflake's abstractions hide it today, but custom-model deployments increasingly touch it.

For infrastructure buyers

  • Add a non-Nvidia line item to your 2026–2027 capacity plan. The "Nvidia or nothing" default is no longer the safe planning assumption it was 12 months ago.
  • Renegotiate Nvidia-based committed-use discounts with this deal as leverage. Hyperscaler sales teams are now competing against in-house silicon, not just against each other.

For investors and analysts

  • Watch Nvidia's Q2 FY27 commentary (August 2026) for any softening of the customer-concentration narrative. One $6B miss is noise; a pattern is signal.
  • Track AWS segment margin: a successful external Trainium contract should show up as operating leverage within 2–3 quarters.
💡
If you run AI workloads on Snowflake today, the practical action this week is small but real: tag your Cortex usage by workload type so you can measure the pricing changes when they arrive. Vendors rarely cut prices uniformly — the savings will land on specific SKUs first.
