What Happened

If you searched "is Claude down" this morning, the honest answer is: not really — at least not right now. Anthropic's status page showed all six monitored components operational as of 12:03 AM UTC on June 22, 2026. The only blemish was a one-minute spike in error rates at 12:38 AM ET affecting Opus 4.8, Opus 4.7, Opus 4.6, and Sonnet 4.6. StatusGator logged just two user outage reports in the prior 24 hours.

So why is the query trending? Because June 2026 has been the worst reliability stretch in Anthropic's history, and users have been conditioned to check. The timeline:

  • June 2: A major global disruption hit Opus 4.6, the Claude API, and the Claude Code CLI with elevated error rates. The reported root cause was a bug in Claude Code's sub-agent system — sub-agents multiplying exponentially and looping infinitely, hammering Anthropic's own backend.
  • June 5: A second significant outage took down claude.ai, Claude Code, and Cowork simultaneously, degrading multiple frontier models.
  • June 16: What outlets called the tenth disruption in roughly 12 days, with Opus 4.8 and Haiku 4.5 errors persisting even after a fix was pushed at 2:00 PM ET.

Anthropic distinguishes two failure signatures: HTTP 500 errors (an internal server failure) and HTTP 529 errors (capacity overload). June saw a lot of both.

💡
Quick check: Before assuming the problem is on your end, load status.claude.com for component-level health, and cross-reference an independent aggregator like StatusGator. A 529 means Anthropic is overloaded — retry with backoff. A 500 means something broke server-side — retrying rarely helps.

Why It Matters

The story isn't one bad day. It's that Claude has quietly become infrastructure, and infrastructure is judged by uptime, not benchmarks. Anthropic has acknowledged that demand is outrunning capacity — driven by Claude Code's explosive growth and a reported 55% jump in Claude mobile app downloads. When the supplier of your coding agent goes dark for the third time in two weeks, "the model is smart" stops being the relevant metric.

Who loses: developers who wired Claude Code into CI pipelines and shipping workflows, and startups whose products are thin wrappers over the Anthropic API with no failover. Who wins: multi-provider routing layers and Anthropic's competitors. Every 529 is a free advertisement for OpenAI's GPT models and Google's Gemini, and for gateway tools that let you swap providers on a bad day.

There's also a trust dimension. Cybernews reported that Anthropic was probing customer data-leak claims following one of the June outages. Whether or not those claims hold up, pairing reliability problems with a data-integrity question is exactly the combination enterprise buyers escalate to their security teams.

Context

This is the flip side of a breakout year. Anthropic shipped fast — Opus 4.6, 4.7, and 4.8 plus Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5 are all in active rotation — and Claude Code became the default agentic coding tool for a large slice of developers. That adoption curve is the proximate cause of the strain: the same sub-agent capability that makes Claude Code powerful is what melted the backend on June 2 when it ran away.

The pattern echoes earlier cloud-era growing pains — the AWS S3 and major Azure incidents that turned "the cloud is down" into a recurring news genre. AI is now entering that genre. The difference worth flagging: Anthropic has not published detailed post-incident root-cause analyses or engineering post-mortems for any of the June disruptions. In the cloud world, public post-mortems became the price of enterprise trust. Anthropic hasn't paid it yet.

⚠️
Don't over-read today. A one-minute blip and two user reports is a normal day for any service at this scale. The signal isn't June 22 — it's the cluster of ten-plus incidents earlier in the month. Treat the trend, not the tick.

What To Do Next

Concrete steps depending on who you are:

  • Developers: Add a provider-agnostic gateway (LiteLLM, OpenRouter, or your own router) so a 529 fails over to Gemini or GPT instead of failing your build. Wrap every Anthropic call in exponential backoff with jitter, and cap retries — a tight retry loop is how you turn their outage into your bill spike.
  • Teams running Claude Code in CI: Make the agent step non-blocking or gated behind a health check on status.claude.com. Don't let a sub-agent outage wedge your pipeline.
  • Buyers and procurement: Ask Anthropic directly for SLA terms, incident-credit policy, and whether post-mortems will be published. The absence of public RCAs is a fair negotiating point right now.
  • Everyone: Keep a fallback model configured and tested before you need it. The cheapest insurance against an AI outage is a second API key you've already validated.

For today specifically: Claude is up. Check the status page, retry with backoff if you hit an error, and budget for the reality that "AI as infrastructure" now includes infrastructure-grade downtime.
