What Happened

The AI industry's two most valuable startups are now racing each other onto the public markets — and this week the pecking order between them got scrambled. On July 3, 2026, multiple outlets reported that Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI on revenue, a milestone that would have looked implausible even a year ago.

The key numbers, as reported by secondary-market trackers and company disclosures:

  • Anthropic reportedly crossed roughly $44 billion in annualized run-rate revenue as of May 2026 and is on track to post its first-ever operating profit — about $559 million — in Q2 2026.
  • OpenAI filed confidential IPO paperwork in June (following Anthropic, which filed roughly a week earlier), working with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, with a listing possible as soon as September 2026.
  • OpenAI's most recent secondary tender priced it near $730 billion; Anthropic's confidential filing reportedly carries a valuation above $900 billion. Consensus IPO targets for OpenAI run to $1 trillion or more.
  • On July 1, OpenAI unveiled its custom "Jalapeño" inference chip, and reporting indicates it has offered the U.S. government a 5% stake as part of a broader deal.

In short: Anthropic is now the revenue leader and closer to profitability, while OpenAI remains the larger private valuation. Both are heading for IPOs within months of each other.

💡
"Run-rate revenue" annualizes the latest month, so it runs ahead of trailing-12-month figures. Treat the $44B and $730B–$900B numbers as fast-moving, secondary-market estimates — not audited results. The audited picture arrives with the S-1 filings.

Why It Matters

For three years the story was "OpenAI, and everyone chasing it." That framing is now wrong. The shift matters for concrete reasons:

Winners

  • Anthropic gets to walk into public markets with the two things investors reward most: revenue leadership and a credible path to profit. An operating profit this quarter is a genuine differentiator in a sector defined by cash burn.
  • Enterprise buyers gain leverage. Two well-capitalized frontier labs competing for the same contracts means better pricing and faster feature parity — see California's statewide deal for Claude at a 50% discount.

Under pressure

  • OpenAI now has to justify a valuation roughly double its revenue lead. The Jalapeño chip and the reported government-stake offer read as moves to shore up cost structure and political standing ahead of an IPO where it will be measured against a profitable rival.
  • Public investors face a genuinely hard question: at $700B–$1T+, how much growth is already priced in? European AI-linked stocks already wobbled this month on exactly this anxiety.

Context

This week's news isn't a bolt from the blue — it's the culmination of a build-up across 2026:

  • Product cadence. On July 1, Anthropic made Claude Sonnet 5 the default for all Free and Pro users, pricing it (through August 31) below the older Sonnet 4.6 while claiming near-Opus 4.8 performance. Cheaper, more capable models are what turn usage into that $44B run-rate.
  • Export-control whiplash. The U.S. Commerce Department lifted controls on Anthropic's Fable 5 on June 30 (imposed June 12), after a new safety classifier reportedly blocked the triggering jailbreak in 99%+ of attempts — a reminder that frontier-model access is now a policy variable.
  • The IPO race itself. Anthropic filing first, then OpenAI, mirrors the competitive leapfrogging seen in the model releases. Both want to plant a flag in public markets before the other sets the valuation anchor.

The regulatory backdrop is tightening in parallel. The White House is in advanced talks with OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic on voluntary AI release standards, and the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance opens in Geneva on July 6 — the day after this publishes.

💡
Watch the S-1 filings, not the headlines. Confidential drafts become public before any roadshow, and that's when revenue mix, gross margins, and compute costs stop being estimates and become numbers you can actually underwrite.

What To Do Next

For developers: Don't assume a single-vendor default anymore. With Sonnet 5 now the free/pro default and priced below its predecessor through Aug 31, re-run your cost-and-quality benchmarks this month — the price/performance ranking that held in Q1 may have flipped. Build an abstraction layer so you can route between OpenAI and Anthropic per task rather than betting an entire stack on one API.

For enterprise buyers: Use the competition. If you're negotiating now, cite the California 50%-discount precedent and ask both vendors for multi-year price protection ahead of their IPOs, when list prices may firm up. Pin down data-handling and model-deprecation terms in writing.

For investors and finance teams: Wait for the S-1s before forming a valuation view. The two data points that matter — gross margin on inference and true customer concentration — are not in the leaked run-rate numbers. Treat any pre-IPO valuation as a negotiating position, not a fact.

For everyone: If you have regulatory exposure, read the outputs of the Geneva dialogue and the White House voluntary-standards talks as they land this month. The rules for frontier-model release are being written right now, in the same weeks these companies file to go public.
