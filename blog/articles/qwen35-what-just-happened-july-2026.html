What Happened

A new medical model called Reasoning-Medical0.1-27B surfaced this week, built as a finetune of Qwen3.5-27B, with its authors claiming it surpasses Google's MedGemma on clinical reasoning. Treat that claim as unverified — it is self-reported, and "surpasses MedGemma" is ambiguous until someone says which MedGemma. Google ships at least three: a 4B multimodal variant, a 27B text-only variant, and a 27B multimodal variant. Beating the text-only 27B on text benchmarks is a very different accomplishment from beating the multimodal one on imaging.

The more durable fact is the base model underneath it. Alibaba's Qwen3.5 family shipped on a staggered schedule earlier this year:

  • February 16, 2026 — the flagship Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, 397B total parameters activating only 17B per token, released on the eve of Chinese New Year. Natively multimodal, 201 languages and dialects (up from 119 in Qwen3), video understanding up to two hours, and a linear-attention plus sparse-MoE architecture aimed squarely at inference cost.
  • February 24, 2026 — the medium series: Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, and the dense Qwen3.5-27B.
  • March 2, 2026 — the small series: 9B, 4B, 2B, and 0.8B, targeted at on-device work.

Nearly all of it is Apache 2.0, including the 397B flagship — an unusually permissive license at that capability tier. Hosted Qwen3.5-Plus runs roughly $0.26 per million input tokens and $1.56 per million output tokens. Self-hosting costs nothing per token.

Why It Matters

Qwen3.5 has been superseded. Alibaba announced Qwen3.6-Plus on April 2, 2026, and Qwen3.7-Max now appears on pricing aggregators. And yet the medical finetune landing this month is built on Qwen3.5-27B, not on anything newer.

That is the story. The frontier and the finetuning substrate have decoupled. Specialist builders are not chasing the newest checkpoint — they are anchoring on the one that is permissively licensed, small enough to train on affordable hardware, and stable enough that the tooling around it has stopped moving. A 27B dense model is the sweet spot: it fits on a single high-memory GPU for inference, it is tractable to finetune without a cluster, and dense beats MoE for finetuning ergonomics because you are not fighting expert-routing collapse on a narrow domain corpus.

💡
The lag is the point. A base model becomes valuable to finetuners roughly one to two generations after release, once quantizations, LoRA recipes, and serving stacks have settled. If you are picking a substrate, the newest model is usually the wrong answer.

Who wins: Alibaba, which converts an Apache 2.0 giveaway into ecosystem gravity — every specialist finetune is a durable dependency on Qwen tokenization, chat templates, and architecture. Also small clinical-AI teams, who can now credibly ship a domain model without Google-scale budgets.

Who loses: Google's positioning for MedGemma. MedGemma's pitch was a strong, health-tuned open model from a trusted lab. If a hobbyist finetune of a general-purpose Chinese base model can plausibly contest it, then the moat was never the medical tuning — it was distribution and trust. Those are worth defending, but they are not the same product.

Context

Qwen3.5's February launch landed in a crowded week of Chinese model releases, with Alibaba explicitly repositioning the family around agents rather than chat. The efficiency numbers were the headline: the 397B flagship was pitched as matching Qwen3-Max — a model exceeding one trillion parameters — while activating 17B per token.

But the more consequential results were further down the range. Reported benchmarks put Qwen3.5-27B at 72.4 on SWE-bench Verified, in the neighborhood of GPT-5 mini, from a fully open-weight dense model. Qwen3.5-9B was reported at 81.7 on GPQA Diamond against GPT-OSS-120B's 71.5, and 70.1 on MMMU-Pro against 59.7. These are vendor-adjacent numbers and deserve the usual skepticism, but the direction is consistent: the capability floor for small open models moved sharply in the first quarter of 2026.

That floor is what makes a credible 27B medical model possible at all. MedGemma exists because Google took Gemma 3 and trained it on medical text and imaging. Reasoning-Medical0.1-27B is the same maneuver, run by a much smaller party, on a base that was free.

What To Do Next

If you are evaluating the medical model: do not deploy on the strength of a benchmark claim. Ask which MedGemma variant it was compared against, on which benchmark, and whether the eval was contaminated by training data. Medical benchmarks like MedQA are heavily represented in web-scraped corpora. Run it against your own held-out cases before it touches anything clinical.

💡
No open-weight medical model is a medical device. Neither MedGemma nor any Qwen finetune is cleared for diagnosis. Benchmark scores describe question-answering, not clinical safety, and the gap between the two is where liability lives.

If you are choosing a base model to finetune: Qwen3.5-27B dense is a defensible pick precisely because it is not new. Weigh it against Qwen3.6-27B, which arrived April 22, and check whether your serving stack and quantization tooling have caught up to the newer generation before you assume newer is better.

If you are buying inference: price the self-hosted path honestly. Apache 2.0 means zero per-token cost, but a 27B dense model at production concurrency is a real GPU bill. At $0.26 and $1.56 per million tokens, hosted Qwen3.5-Plus is cheap enough that self-hosting only pays off at sustained volume or under a data-residency requirement — which, for clinical workloads, is often exactly the requirement you have.
