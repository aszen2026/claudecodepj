The Bot That Stopped Joining Your Calls

The biggest shift in AI meeting notes over the past 18 months isn't smarter summaries — it's the quiet disappearance of the meeting bot. In 2024, the awkward "Otter.ai Notetaker has joined" pop-up was everywhere. By early 2026, a growing share of tools capture audio directly from your machine, no third participant required. That single change has reshuffled the entire category, because nobody wants to explain to a client why a robot is silently sitting in the room.

I tested seven of the most-used tools across roughly 40 real meetings — sales calls, standups, messy three-person brainstorms, and one genuinely terrible call with overlapping accents and a dog barking. Here's what actually held up.

The Contenders

Granola — the desktop-native favorite

Granola listens through your computer's audio, merges what it hears with the notes you type, and produces a cleaned-up version seconds after you hang up. No bot, no calendar-wide announcement. In my testing it was the most accurate on chaotic multi-speaker calls, partly because it leans on your typed shorthand to anchor the summary. The catch: it's macOS-first, Windows support is still maturing, and there's no automatic recording archive unless you enable it. At $18/user/month, it's priced for individuals and small teams, not 5,000-seat enterprises.

Fireflies.ai — the integration workhorse

Fireflies remains the tool to beat if your notes need to go somewhere — Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack, Notion. Its "Fred" assistant can answer questions across your entire meeting history ("what did the Acme team say about pricing in Q1?"), and the search is genuinely good. Transcription accuracy sat around 91% on clean audio in my tests but dropped noticeably with heavy accents. The free tier caps you at 800 minutes of storage, which fills fast.

Fathom — the generous free tier

Fathom built its reputation on a free plan that doesn't feel crippled: unlimited recording, transcription, and summaries for individuals. The AI-generated action items are reliably formatted, and the 30-second post-call summary is the fastest I measured. It's the easiest tool to recommend to someone who has never used one. Team features and CRM sync live behind the $19/month paid tier.

Circleback — the action-item specialist

Where most tools produce a wall of summary text, Circleback obsesses over what happens next. It extracts tasks, assigns owners, and can auto-trigger workflows — create a Linear ticket, send a follow-up email, update a CRM field. If your meetings exist to produce decisions, this is the sharpest tool in the roundup. It's also the least useful if you just want a clean transcript to skim.

Otter.ai — the incumbent

Otter is still here, still solid, and still the default at many large organizations. Live transcription during the call is its standout feature for accessibility. But it has been slower to drop the bot-joiner model, and its summaries felt the most generic of the group — accurate, but rarely surfacing the one detail that mattered.

💡
Test on your worst call, not your best one. Every tool nails a quiet two-person Zoom. The real differences show up on a five-person call with cross-talk, a shared screen, and someone dialing in from a car. Run a trial on the messiest meeting you have this week.

What the Marketing Won't Tell You

Three things became obvious only after weeks of daily use:

  • Accuracy claims are inflated. Vendors quote 95%+ transcription accuracy. That number holds for studio-quality audio. On real calls with cross-talk and accents, expect 85–92% — still useful, but proofread anything you forward to a client.
  • The summary, not the transcript, is the product. Every tool transcribes adequately now. The differentiator is whether the AI surfaces the one decision buried in 45 minutes of rambling. This is where Granola and Circleback pull ahead.
  • Privacy is a real decision, not a checkbox. Bot-free tools that record local audio keep less data on vendor servers, but check whether transcripts are used for model training. Several enterprise buyers I spoke with rejected otherwise-strong tools on this point alone.
💡
Tell people they're being recorded. Bot-free capture removes the visible cue that a meeting is being transcribed. In two-party-consent regions that's a legal issue, not a courtesy. A one-line heads-up at the start of the call covers you and keeps trust intact.

The Recommendation

If you're a solo founder, consultant, or developer who lives in back-to-back calls, start with Granola — the bot-free workflow and sharp summaries justify the price, and the trial is risk-free. If your notes need to flow into a CRM and you want searchable history across a whole team, Fireflies is the safer institutional bet. On a tight budget or just getting started, Fathom's free tier is the most generous on-ramp in the category. And if your meetings exist purely to generate decisions and follow-ups, give Circleback a week — it's the only tool here that treats the action item, not the transcript, as the whole point. Whichever you pick, judge it on your ugliest call, not the demo.
