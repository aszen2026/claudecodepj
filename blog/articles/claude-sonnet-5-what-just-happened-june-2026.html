Why It Matters

Sonnet is the tier most teams actually run in production. Opus is the expensive flagship reserved for the hardest tasks; Haiku is the cheap, fast option for high-volume simple calls. Sonnet sits in the middle and absorbs the bulk of real workloads — coding agents, RAG pipelines, document processing, customer-facing assistants. A new Sonnet generation moves the default, so its impact is broader than a flagship Opus bump.

Who benefits if the release lands as expected:

  • Developers on the 4.x Sonnet line get a likely capability lift on the tier they already budget for.
  • Agent and tool-use builders care most — Sonnet is where the price-to-capability ratio makes long agentic loops affordable.
  • Anthropic gains a fresh competitive anchor against OpenAI's GPT line and Google's Gemini in the mid-tier, where volume and margin live.

Who faces pressure: anyone who hard-coded claude-sonnet-4-6 into pinned integrations, and competitors whose mid-tier pricing was calibrated against the current Sonnet. A version increment usually resets the comparison table the whole market shops against.

That made the lineup capable but harder to reason about: a 4.8 Opus and a 4.6 Sonnet do not obviously belong to the same release wave. A "Sonnet 5" label is cleaner, and it sets up the expectation that Opus and Haiku 5 follow on the same generational track.

The pattern of discovery is also familiar.
